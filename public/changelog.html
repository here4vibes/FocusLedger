October 2026
07 Oct
"Let it go" instead of "permanently delete"
Removing a task used to throw up a blunt "This can't be undone — permanently delete?" wall. Now the button says "Let it go," the task quietly fades out, and a "Keep it" option lingers for a few seconds in case you change your mind — no scary modal, no shame. Letting something go should feel like relief, not a verdict. This maps to rejection sensitivity (RSD) from the science page — a punitive, irreversible-feeling UI is exactly what makes an ADHD brain avoid tidying up at all.
Improvement
07 Oct
One "today" — the radar is home, the timeline is a tap away
There used to be two screens both calling themselves "today," which is one too many when you're deciding where to even look. Now your home screen's radar — the top 3 — is the single source of truth, and the full hour-by-hour day (with how long things really take) is a clearly-labeled drill-in you open with "Full day," then step back home from. One place to land, one place to go deeper. Fewer "where am I supposed to be?" moments is the whole point for an ADHD brain.
Improvement
07 Oct
The little "done" burst, everywhere now
That satisfying particle burst when you finish a task used to only appear on the tasks page. Now it fires wherever you complete something — finishing a task in conversation with Buddy, checking off a single step, and clearing your whole spending check-in. The reward is the point: immediate, visible payoff on completion is one of the strongest levers for an ADHD brain against delay aversion — the "why bother, the reward's too far off" feeling. This maps to the dopamine and reward timing research on the science page — making the finish feel good, right now, is what makes the next start easier.
Improvement
07 Oct
One gold, one coral — a calmer palette
The app's accent color used to be gold on most screens but coral on a couple, so the same brand kept changing character as you moved around. Now gold is the single brand accent everywhere, and coral is reserved for one job only — your money and spending. Fewer competing colors means less to process on every screen, which matters when attention is the scarce resource.

A reminder you can count on arriving is the whole foundation; a flaky one is worse than none.

Habit stacking — anchor tasks to routines + Buddy detects co-occurring task pairs
Tasks can now declare "do this after [Routine]" — open any task in the expanded detail view and pick a routine from the "Do after" dropdown. When you mark that routine complete, FocusLedger sends a push notification reminding you to do the anchored task next. Separately, the weekly pattern detection job now scans your task history for sequential pairs: if you complete Task B within 60 minutes of Task A on 3 or more distinct days, Buddy surfaces a "Habit stack detected 🔗" card with a one-tap "Stack them" to accept it. This maps to implementation intentions from the science page — stimulus-response links that route around the executive function gap and make follow-through automatic.

16 Jun
Security — Plaid webhook signature verification
Bank transaction webhooks from Plaid are now cryptographically verified before processing. Each webhook carries an ES256-signed JWT; the app validates the signature against Plaid's public key, checks the body hash, and rejects anything older than 5 minutes. Previously any POST to the webhook URL could trigger a bank sync — now only genuine Plaid events are processed.
